---
baslik: The Mirror and the Window
---
When I was twenty, the first thing I looked for when I walked into a room was the most beautiful face. In a crowd my eyes always drifted there. To where the light fell most, to where everyone was looking. When I found that face, something stirred in me. I thought it was love. Yet that stirring was, most of the time, no different from standing in front of a shop window.

Looking at a beautiful face is like looking at a landscape. Your eyes rest. For a moment the world seems more orderly, more harmonious. Then you walk on. Behind you no name, no voice, no memory remains. Only a glimmer. When I was young I cared a great deal about that glimmer. Because I thought it was a door. I believed that if I passed through that door, everything would change.

Then one day I noticed that looking is one thing and wanting another. I didn't want to come close to every face I found beautiful. Sometimes the most ordinary smile, a way of walking, a momentary drop of the shoulders drew me more. The skin spoke another language. The eye another. I had always assumed they were saying the same thing.

Later a third thing appeared. Wanting to know someone. Being curious about their mornings. Wanting to know at which song they fall silent, at which word their eyes fill. This was the wish of neither the eye nor the skin. It came from deeper. It was like opening a window onto a stranger's life.

And last came the thing understood latest of all. Being able to walk together. Being able to drink tea at the same table after a quarrel. Being able to be silent side by side in a hospital corridor. This was not really a feeling. It was something that formed slowly, patiently, within time. No one could see it at first glance. No face, however beautiful, could promise it in advance.

When I was young I thought these four were one thing. If beautiful, then attractive; if attractive, then the right person. I looked at the first step of a staircase and thought I had reached the top.

Now, looking back, I see something else too. When I was searching for those beautiful faces, I wasn't really searching for them. I was searching for myself. If a beautiful person chose me, I would be valuable. If a beautiful person looked at me with desire, I would be someone worthy of being wanted. If someone everyone admired walked beside me, I would gain a place in the crowd's gaze. Those faces were mirrors to me. Looking at them, I was trying to see my own face.

A mirror has a bad side. It always shows you yourself. Not the person in front of you. It does not show their tiredness, their fear, that small hurt left over from childhood. It only shows how you look beside them. A person cannot love by looking in a mirror. They can only wait to be approved.

As the years pass, a place inside you quietly fills. You know a little better who you are, what you love, where you stand. You no longer have to read your worth from someone else's face. That is when the mirror slowly turns into a window. When you look, you see not yourself but them.

And looking through that window, beauty does not disappear. It only changes place. Once it was in the face itself; now it is in how that face looks at you. Once it was in the line of the body; now it is in the way that body leans toward you on a cold evening. The smile of the person you will love may not be the most beautiful smile in the world. But when that smile turns to you, it gives something no shop window can give.

Perhaps that is why old couples look at each other the way they do. Through the wrinkles, the white hair, the slowing steps. Someone looking from outside may see no beauty there. But they see it. Because years ago they put down the mirror and opened the window. They chose each other not to be approved, but simply to see.

I am not angry at the glimmer of youth. Without that glimmer perhaps I would never have walked into any room, never searched for any face. But now when I walk into a room I look not for the most beautiful face but for the warmest gaze. Not where the light falls most, but the face that lights up when it sees me.

Perhaps beauty was a door. But the real home begins after the door.
