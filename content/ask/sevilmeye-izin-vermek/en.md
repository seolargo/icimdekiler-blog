---
baslik: Letting Yourself Be Loved
---
In a song, a woman asks her lover: Who has loved you in this world? She answers herself: Only me. Then she adds: And I have been loved more than enough. Two short sentences, two separate lives. One grew up inside love, the other on its shore. And now these two people have met inside the same love.

A love like this would probably be very beautiful but very hard. Because the two would live the same thing from entirely different places.

For someone who has been deeply loved, love is like a mother tongue. They do not even remember learning it. Saying "I love you" is not hard. Embracing, paying attention, trusting are all familiar gestures. Such a person knows that love, like a spring, will always fill up again. That is why they are not afraid to give. Giving does not diminish them, because life has taught them that love does not run out.

For someone who has never been loved, love is a foreign language. They hear its words but cannot quite make out their meaning. When someone comes to them with love, at first they do not know what to do. Sometimes they do not believe it and ask, "Why does she love me?" Sometimes, deep down, they expect it to end one day, because no love in their life has ever lasted. Strange but true: for such a person, receiving love is even harder than giving it. Giving is doing something. Receiving is being left defenceless.

At first the man would be astonished. For the first time in his life someone would have told him, "I am enough." Like at the end of the song, he would see a gull flying fearlessly over the sea, a child running as if in paradise, and perhaps for the first time he would truly laugh. For him it would be almost a miracle. Like something he had never known suddenly resting in the palms of his hands.

But miracles are frightening too. When you come to have something you never had, the fear of losing it comes with it. The man might grow jealous, test the woman, ask her constantly for proof. He might ask "Do you really love me?" in a different way every day. Or, the opposite, he might pull back out of fear and build a wall. Because the easiest way to lose something is never to fully have it.

The woman, for her part, would sometimes grow tired. Even when the love she gave was returned, it would not come back in the same language. The man would show his love not with words but with quiet things. A glass of tea, a door that has been fixed, a hand held out at an unexpected moment. Perhaps sometimes he could not show it at all. And the woman would have to learn to look for love inside that silence.

This love had one more danger. Over time the woman could become less a lover than a mother, a shelter. The huge arms and huge hands of the song, someone pressed to the chest so that he will not be cold, will not tremble. It is a very tender picture. But if it goes on too long, the relationship loses its balance. One becomes the one who always gives, the other the one who always takes. And one day the one who always gives begins to ask who will carry her.

Still, if it went well, each would learn something from the other. Slowly the man would learn to receive love. First with suspicion, then out of habit, and finally with trust. And one day, though no one had taught him, he would begin to give love too. Even if in his own quiet language. The woman would understand that love is not this easy for everyone. She would see that some people learn love later, with effort, like a foreign language. Perhaps for the first time she would realise what great luck it was to have what she had.

Perhaps this is the real exchange in a love like this. One teaches how to love. The other reminds how great a thing it is to be loved. One brings the abundance, the other the value of that abundance.

But for this to happen, both of them must do something. The woman must be patient. Her love will not be understood at once, will not be returned at once. The man must be brave. Brave, because for someone who has never been loved, the greatest risk is neither losing nor being abandoned. The greatest risk is letting yourself be loved. Because the moment he allows it, he will also know fully that once, he was not loved.
