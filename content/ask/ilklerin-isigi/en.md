---
baslik: The Light of Firsts
---
One morning you open the curtains and outside everything is white. You are seeing snow for the first time in your life. You rest your forehead against the cold glass. Your breath fogs the window, you wipe it, you look again. That day, snow is the most miraculous thing in the world. Years later it snows again. This time the first things that come to mind are the roads, the traffic, wet shoes. The snow has not changed. What has changed is the eye looking at it.

Firsts have a light of their own. Most of the time that light does not come from the thing itself. It comes from the fact that we are looking at it for the first time.

Think of the day you first saw the sea. That blueness stretching all the way to where the horizon ends. The first wave touching your feet. The salt water that got into your mouth. Afterwards you go into the sea hundreds of times. You see lovelier coves, clearer waters. But none of them gets under your skin like that first wave. Because that day the sea was not just the sea. It was the moment you first learned the world was bigger than you thought.

The day you earned your own money for the first time is the same. Maybe it was a small amount. Maybe today it would not even cover a dinner. But when you held it in your hand it had a different kind of weight. That money was telling you something. Now you can stand on your own feet, it said. The salaries that came later were bigger, but none of them ever made that voice heard so clearly again.

The night you first set foot in a new city. You do not know the street names, you do not know what time the bakery on the corner opens in the morning. Everything is a little frightening, a little enchanting. The lights burn differently, people walk differently. A few years later that city becomes yours. You know the shortcuts, you know your favourite café. The city opens itself to you, but the spell of that first night never returns. You came to love it as you got to know it, but on the first night you had loved it without knowing it.

Sometimes you wonder. Was that first snow really the most beautiful snow? Was that first sea really the bluest sea? Most likely not. Maybe that snow melted quickly, maybe that sea was murky. But a first encounter does not see the flaws. With nothing to compare against, everything is whole. The eye has not yet learned to measure; it only looks. And only looking is the purest way a person can love.

Then we grow up. We learn to measure. This coffee is better than that one, this city is lovelier than that one, this song is the same as the last. As knowledge grows, wonder shrinks. Sometimes this feels like a loss. As if the world had taken something back from us.

But perhaps the firsts took nothing from us. Perhaps they only left a trace. If today we can still look at something with admiration, we owe it to those first snows, those first waves. They taught us how to look. They wrote on our hearts what it feels like to wonder.

And sometimes, however rarely, that feeling comes back. On a street you have walked for years, the light falls differently. In a song you have heard a thousand times, you notice a note you had never heard before. Looking at a face you see every day, you suddenly feel as if you are seeing it for the first time. For a moment the world is new again.

That is when you understand. Firsts are not lived only once on the calendar. Every moment you truly look at something, with all your attention, with all your heart, is a first. The point is not that things are new, but that we can look at them with new eyes.

Perhaps the firsts were beautiful not because they were good but because they were first. Yet the gift they left us was real. They taught us to keep, somewhere inside, the eye that looks at the world for the first time. And once that eye has opened, a person waits, now and then until the end of their life, to wonder again.
