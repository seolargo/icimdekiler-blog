---
baslik: Beside a Fire That Won't Burn
---
Imagine lighting a fire. A winter night, a cold room. A match in your hand, wood in front of you. You strike the match, the flame touches the tip of the wood. For a moment a spark flares, then dies. You strike another match. You blow. You lean in close, you give it your breath. Smoke rises, your eyes sting. But the fire does not catch.

Because the wood is wet.

I have lived this many times. I sat across from a woman and tried to light a fire inside me. Alone. I wrote to her, she answered, but she never started. I suggested a place, she found a reason, but never suggested another place. I arranged a meeting, she came, but it was always me who asked about the next one. And each time I struck one more match.

I loaded a meaning onto her every silence. Maybe she's busy, I said. Maybe that's just how she is, I said. Maybe she'll open up in time, I said. When she sent an emoji, I hoped for days. When she wrote a short reply, I searched for love inside that shortness. When I didn't find it, I kept searching.

After a while I began to make myself smaller. I became someone who asked less, wanted less, took up less space. So as not to scare her off. So she wouldn't close the door completely. But the door had never opened. I had only learned to stand more quietly on its threshold.

Later I learned what this is called. Trying to force a relationship into being.

When I first heard it, something in me ached. Because it sounded like a bad thing. As if I were a pushy person, as if I had imposed myself on someone. But it isn't. Trying to force it is not a flaw. It comes from hope. It is a heart that has waited a long time clinging to the first light that appears before it. It is not being able to let go, because giving up feels like saying "it didn't work again." It is the story built in the head being so beautiful that you cannot accept that reality does not fit it.

So I am not angry with myself. I only see the difference now.

Giving effort is something else.

Giving effort is throwing wood onto a fire that is already burning. There is a flame there, however small. You throw on a branch, they throw on a branch. The fire grows, the room warms, and you both hold your hands out to it. Perhaps you get tired, you get up and carry wood, but there is a fullness inside you. Because everything you give comes back to you as warmth.

Trying to force it is kneeling in front of wet wood and blowing alone. Your breath runs out, the fire doesn't catch. And you blame the fire not burning on your own breath not being enough. You think you need to blow harder. But the problem is not the breath. It is the wood.

From the outside the two look alike. In both you write, you offer, you try. The difference is not in what you do. The difference is in what comes back from the other side. And in what you feel inside.

In a relationship where you give effort, if you stop one day, the other person carries on. They write, they call, they say "where are you?" In a relationship you are trying to force, everything stops the moment you stop. The phone goes quiet. It goes dark as if the fire had never burned.

Knowing this hurts. But at the same time it sets you free.

Because I know now that trying a little too hard at first is normal. Some people are shy, some open up slowly. You can't know without trying. Striking a match once or twice is hope. But always striking it alone is wearing yourself out. The point is not whether to try or not. The point is knowing when to let go.

And letting go is not giving up. Letting go is saving your breath for the right place.

One day I will find dry wood. Or it will find me. Perhaps I will strike the match, but she will lean in and blow too. Our two breaths will join, and the flame will rise on its own, without forcing. That night the room will warm. We will hold our hands out to the fire. And for the first time I will feel that I am not trying to light a fire alone.

I will give effort to make that fire grow. I will carry wood, get up in the night to stir its ashes, wait beside it so it won't go out. But none of this will tire me. Because she will be waiting with me.

A relationship happens when two people walk toward each other. If something is going to happen, I won't need to force it alone. If it isn't, however hard I blow, wet wood will not burn.

I am saving my breath now for the one I will blow with.
