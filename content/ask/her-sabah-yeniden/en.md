baslik: Again Every Morning
---
There are two ways of staying in a relationship. Either out of need or out of desire. The one who stays out of need says, "I can't do without them." The one who stays out of desire says, "I could do without them, but I want to be with them." At first sight the two look alike. Both wake up in the same house, both sit at the same table. But there is a deep difference between them. Need ends one day. The wound closes, the problem is solved, the person grows stronger. On that day the reason for the bond disappears too. Desire does not depend on a reason. Two strong people can choose to stand side by side without needing each other. That is what is called a choice.

A choice is not one big "yes" given on a single day. It is a small "you again" repeated every morning. You wake up and say good morning to the person beside you. You argued yesterday, but today you want to talk to them again. You could have left, but you stay. Most of the time this is quiet, almost unnoticed. But on hard days it becomes deliberate. The morning after a fight, in the middle of a dull week, in a period when the excitement has faded. In those moments, saying "I choose you anyway" is what keeps the relationship standing. Choice takes the relationship out of obligation. No one is chained to anyone. The door is open. And staying inside means something precisely because of that.

So why should anyone choose to wake up beside a woman, beside a man, every morning? A list could be made in answer. Depth, trust, support, a shared life, growing old together. All of it is true. But none of it keeps a person in that bed every morning. No one loves by being persuaded by a list. What keeps a person there is much simpler. Seeing that face when you wake puts you at ease. You want to tell them about your day first. Even being silent beside them is lovely. A morning without them feels as if something is missing. When a choice is a duty, it is heavy. When it is a desire, it is light. It is hard to explain this feeling to someone who does not yet know it. Like explaining to someone who has never been in the sea why people run to it every summer.

That is why the question "why should I choose" is not a shameful one. Perhaps the heart has not yet met someone who will make it feel this desire. Or perhaps attachment recalls something learned long ago: carrying someone, getting lost inside someone, giving yourself up. Then waking up beside someone every morning naturally feels heavy. But in a healthy bond, waking up beside someone every morning is not giving yourself up. It is waking up as yourself and having someone you love beside you as well. And not choosing is an honest choice too. No one has to do this. But if one day someone silences that question on their own, that is the choice.

When imagining such a love, people sometimes want the flawless. Someone without wounds. Someone who can love deeply, fall deeply in love, think of nothing but you. The dream is lovely, but there are two knots in it. First, a person without wounds hardly exists. What matters is not the absence of wounds but that the person knows their wound, tends to it, and does not load it onto the other. Two wounded but aware people can be very good for each other. Second, the wish for someone who "will think of nothing but me" is the idea of putting someone at the centre turned inside out. This time it is being the centre that is wanted. In the first days this feeling comes anyway, and it is beautiful. But if it lasts, the other person's own world is erased and the weight of their whole universe falls on one person's shoulders. Perhaps this is lovelier: not someone who thinks of nothing but you, but someone whose heart is with you even when their mind is on other things.

So who is it? Someone at peace with themselves. Someone with their own life, work, friends and passions. Someone who does not depend entirely on you to be happy, but is happier with you. Who is not waiting to be rescued. Who can say what they feel, who talks instead of sulking when angry, who can apologise when needed. Whose words and actions stand in the same place. Beside whom you do not wonder, "Does she love me?" Who helps you grow, and is not afraid of you having your own space too. Close to you in values, laughing at the same places in humour.

But written out like this, the profile looks cold. It reads like a job advertisement. And a voice inside says, "That would just be a good partner. It wouldn't be a great love." That feeling is very honest. Because the heart may be used to mistaking intensity for love. The storm created by rescuing, by uncertainty, by being needed sounds like the voice of a great love. Because a healthy person does not create that storm, they seem dull at first. Yet that dullness is very often peace. A heart unused to peace can mistake it for emptiness.

And there is this: no one falls in love with a profile. They fall in love with a person. Those cold words on paper come alive in a real person. In their smile, their voice, their gaze, in moments of silence shared. A great love, too, is usually great not at first sight but over time. Someone who today looks like "just a partner" slowly becomes the love of your life when they stand by you on a hard day, when they see you at your worst and love you still, when shared memories gather over the years. And peace is not the absence of passion. Passion felt for someone you trust is freer and deeper than passion felt for someone you fear.

While thinking about all this, a face sometimes comes to mind. Perhaps a singer, perhaps a writer, perhaps someone you have never met but admire from afar. The face that came to my mind was Carla Bruni's. A woman who writes her own songs, who has her own light, who stands in no one's shadow. Her voice is calm but her image is passionate. Someone who once openly said she was bored with monogamy, then met someone, married within a short time, and has kept that choice for years. In other words, someone who once said "why should I choose" and in the end chose. Of course she came to mind not as a person but as a symbol. We see famous people only from the outside, at their most beautiful. But the symbol was saying something: the woman being looked for was not just a healthy partner. She was someone with her own light, with an art or a passion, someone who both calms and enchants. That was the colour missing from the cold part of the profile.

In the end everything leads to the same place. Love does not end with finding someone. It begins again every morning. Not out of need, but out of desire. Not by obligation, but freely. And perhaps the most beautiful love is being able to say, with the door wide open, "you again," every morning anew.
