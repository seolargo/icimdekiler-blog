---
baslik: Someone
---
Imagine a room late at night. The lights are off; only the yellow light of the streetlamp seeps through the curtain. On nights like this a person whispers the same thing to themselves: I wish there were someone.

This sentence is very easy to say. There is no name in it, no face. There is only an empty space. Hearing someone breathe beside you, waiting for a message to arrive, having someone to tell when the day is over. We all want this. And when we say it, we are not lying.

But something strange happens. People come into our lives. Good people, warm people, people who truly put effort into us. And we leave the door ajar but don't fully let them in. Then one day someone comes. Perhaps not the best, perhaps not the easiest either. But when they come, something stirs inside us. And we throw the door wide open.

Why them?

Because what we are looking for is not really a person but a feeling. Breathing easily beside someone for the first time. Dropping the act. Your shoulders loosening without your noticing. Carrying someone's presence inside you the way you carry a house key in your pocket. Not everyone can give this. Sometimes a very good person makes you feel nothing. Sometimes a single glance from a stranger touches a place no one has touched in years.

Being seen is like this too. At a crowded table, with everyone talking, someone looking only at you. Listening to what you say, and sensing what you cannot say. In that moment a person counts themselves as truly existing for the first time. And they become attached to that person. Because the eye that sees you is no longer an ordinary eye.

Then there is aliveness. Your heart speeding up for a moment when the phone buzzes. An ordinary Tuesday evening feeling like a holiday because they came. Streets, cafés, songs suddenly carrying other meanings. Once a person has tasted that feeling, they cannot swap the one who gives it for someone else.

But the quietest of all is familiarity. Sometimes we think we chose someone because they felt right. Yet that person only felt familiar. They resemble the love we learned long ago, as children. Sometimes that familiarity is a shelter. Sometimes it leads us back to a pain we know. Still, it feels right. Because people would rather walk a known road than an unknown happiness.

And this is where our words and our actions part ways.

"I'm open to love," we say. We mean it when we say it. But when someone truly comes close, we step back. Because closeness is as frightening as it is beautiful. Letting someone in means letting in the chance of being broken too. Words are cheap; they cost nothing. But opening the door takes courage. It takes time, it takes pride, sometimes it takes giving up the comfort of a loneliness we have grown used to.

That is why we smile at many people but give our heart to only one. This is not unfairness. The heart simply has a limit. It cannot open to everyone at the same depth. And we do this for the one person we find worth that risk.

Perhaps our most honest self is not what we say but what we do. Not whom we tell "I'm waiting for you," but whose message we wait for at night. Not whom we say we are open to, but for whom we dare the fear. A person never fully knows themselves. But their actions quietly tell them every day who they are.

Then that room again. That yellow light again, that night again. The sentence "I wish there were someone" now carries another meaning. Because now we know that what we want is not just anyone. What we want is that one person beside whom we feel safe, seen and alive.

And when that person comes, however afraid we are, we open the door. Because some doors are knocked on only once.
