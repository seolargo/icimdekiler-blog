baslik: On Being in Love with Love
---
Some people love a person. Others love loving itself. From the outside the second looks a little suspect. As if something were missing, as if the other person were only a pretext. Yet being in love with love may be the state of those who take love most seriously.

Think of it: the first glance, the first message, something stirring inside you every time you look at your phone. The streets look a different colour. Songs suddenly seem written for you. Sleeplessness is not tiredness but a kind of intoxication. This is exactly the state that a person in love with love loves. It reminds them that they are alive.

That is why it would be unfair to tell them, "You don't really love anyone." Each time, they truly love. The other person's smile, their voice, their odd habits. Their love is not false; it is only not lasting. It is like a season. When it comes it covers the whole world, and when it goes it leaves a trace behind.

The greatest enemy of the person in love with love is habit. When routine takes the place of excitement, something inside them goes out. Others call this dimming maturity. "Now it's calm, now it's safe," they say. But they sense a kind of death in that calm. The beginning of the road is bright, the middle is dark. And rather than walk in the dark, they choose to go back to the start.

Is this a weakness or a kind of honesty? Perhaps both. Society tells us that the only right form of love is permanence. Marriage, a home, growing old together. But does everyone have to love the same way? Some people love like a long river, slow and patient. Others like lightning, brief and bright. It makes no sense to reproach lightning for not flowing like a river.

History is full of people in love with love. A great many poems, songs and letters were left by them. Because love is told most by those who seek it most. The one who has found a person falls silent; the one still in pursuit of love writes. Perhaps being in love with love is the name of a search that never ends.

But this road has its price too. Every beginning brings an ending. Every ending its own mourning. The person in love with love loves deeply, then grieves deeply, then loves deeply again. This cycle both keeps them alive and wears them out. Sometimes, at some hour of the night, in the middle of a song, a face from the past comes to mind. In that moment they understand that everyone they loved has taken up a small room inside them. They have gone, but not entirely.

And then there are the others. The person loved by someone in love with love sometimes believes that the season will last forever. Here the only moral limit of being in love with love appears: honesty. Someone who knows their own nature and does not hide it deceives no one. But someone who promises permanence and passes like a season leaves nothing behind but hurt.

Perhaps the real question is not, "Do you love the person or love itself?" Perhaps the real question is how fully you are there in the moment you love. The person in love with love is wholly present in that moment. They do not think of tomorrow, do not calculate, do not hold themselves back. This is something that even lasting loves often forget.

In the end everyone lives their own way of loving. Some knock on a single door for a lifetime. Some on a new door every spring. Both are faces of love. The one in love with love may never arrive anywhere. But they know better than anyone what it means to be on the road, to begin again and again, to love each time as if loving for the first time.
