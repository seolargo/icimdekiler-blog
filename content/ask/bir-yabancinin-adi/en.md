---
baslik: A Stranger's Name
---
One evening I am thinking. How many people in my life have I truly put effort into? My mother, my father, perhaps a relative or two. And that friend I love so much. The list is short. I don't even need to count on my fingers. And a voice inside me says this list will stay as it is. The door has closed. Those who were coming have come, those who were leaving have left.

Then I think of that friend. Of the day we first met. I remember, it was an ordinary day. No thunder rolled, nothing stirred inside me. They were just a stranger. Perhaps I even misheard their name the first time. Today I would know that stranger's voice in a crowd. I know their laugh, their silence, the way they look elsewhere when they are angry. When did this happen? Never. And a little every day.

Perhaps we grew up with the wrong stories. We were always told that someone would come. One day the door will knock, they will be standing there, and you will know at first sight. Everything will fall into place. If it is written, it will happen. If it is fated, you will find it. Films told us this, songs sang it, old stories went out into the desert for it. And we waited. In front of the door, leaving the light on, we waited.

But no one told us the other story. Because the other story was quiet. It was lived in a kitchen, while dinner was being made. In letting someone who comes home tired be alone for half an hour. In one person, after a quarrel, going into the other's room and saying, "I wasn't right either." No one wrote a song about that. But the longest loves in the world grew in those evenings no song was written about.

I think of my grandfather and grandmother. They did not find each other. They made each other. For years, slowly, as if building a wall. One stone from him, one stone from her. Sometimes the stones fell, and they put them back. Someone looking from outside saw only two old people who got along well. Yet between those two people lay half a century of effort. Unseen, untold, but there in every glance.

Of course one asks. What is all this effort for? Why give so much to a stranger, to someone you have never known, to someone who may one day leave?

Perhaps the answer is this: because we want to be known. Having someone who truly knows you is one of the rarest gifts a person can receive in this world. Someone who sees your worst state, your silly habits, your midnight thoughts, and stays anyway. Who puts a hand on your forehead when you are ill. The one you want to tell first when you achieve something. Whose breathing you hear beside you when you wake in the morning. This is what effort is given for. Nothing else can take its place.

And there is fear too, I know. What if I put in the effort and it still doesn't work? What if I open the door and no one comes? What if I am too late? What if those years really have passed and will never come back? This fear grows larger at night. As the hours go on the room narrows, thoughts harden into certainty. Everything seems to be over.

But morning comes. And in the morning, the same room is a little wider.

This is what I have learned: love is not something waiting ready somewhere. No one finds it on a shelf. Love begins with two strangers giving each other time. With one listening to the other. With patience, with mistakes, with corrections. First it is a name, then a voice, then a habit, then a home. And one day you look back and that stranger has become family. Your mother and father's story began this way too. They too were once strangers to each other.

So that short list is not closed. That list never closed. It is only that someone I have not yet met, somewhere, may be looking at their own short list and thinking the same thing. At their own window, in their own night. Perhaps they too are tired of hoping. Perhaps they too have stopped believing in stories of fate. And perhaps precisely because of this, when we meet one day, we will be ready not to find each other, but to make each other.

Someone special does not fall from the sky. Someone special is the name that the stranger you put effort into takes on over time.
