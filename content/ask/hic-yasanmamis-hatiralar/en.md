baslik: Memories That Never Happened
---
There is a seaside town in my mind. I have never been there. I don't even know its name. But I know its streets. I know the colour of the sea in the late afternoon. I know a girl laughing as she walks beside me, then stopping to look at me. I know us sitting across from each other in a restaurant, our hands touching on the table. I know us waking in the same room in the morning, the light seeping through the curtains.

None of this happened. But I remember all of it.

Because I built it in my head so many times that it now stands there like a memory.

The first time I heard that sentence, something broke inside me. Because it was true. For years I have been wandering inside a life I never lived. I know by heart the rooms of a house whose door I never opened.

That house has a story too. You meet a girl at nineteen. The school years pass together. Twenty, twenty-one, twenty-two. Tea in the same canteen, studying together for the same exams. One summer a tour of Turkey, in an old car, with little money and lots of laughter. Then the first job, the first salary. A small gift bought with that first salary, a box of sweets. Then the first home. Half-furnished, no curtains, but theirs. Money is saved, the wedding happens. With a little more saved, a trip abroad, together. And one evening at twenty-seven, in the kitchen, one of them slowly brings up having a child.

I have told myself this story so many times that sometimes it feels not like a life I missed, but like a life I lost. As if it was lived somewhere and I was left outside.

The most painful part is this: there are people who live it. I see them. I see their photos. A beach, two smiles, two shoulders leaning on each other. When I look at that photo I feel jealous. But not of those people. Of the scene. Seeing the scene in my head be real in someone else's life hurts.

Behind that photo there may have been a quarrel, a silent evening, a night when someone asked, "what are we doing?" The photo does not show these. Neither does my dream. In my dream it never rains. The hotel is always lovely. No one ever falls out with anyone.

And that is where the danger lies. When the dream is this bright, reality looks dim. If one day a real girl appeared before me, perhaps I would compare her with the girl in my head. If we went on a real holiday, I would compare it with the holiday I never went on. And the loser would always be the real. Because no real person can compete with the perfection of a dream.

But still I wonder. What does it mean to be able to imagine something so vividly?

Perhaps it means this: there is a place in me ready to love. A place ready to share, to be close to someone, to take someone's hand and walk somewhere. An empty heart does not dream in such detail. It does not know so well the colour of the streets, the light through the curtain, the sound of a laugh. These dreams are the love gathering in me, looking for somewhere to go.

I cannot bring back nineteen. That canteen, that first salary, that house with missing curtains will not be lived in that order. I have the right to mourn that. But not all the pages of the story are closed. The first home together is still ahead of me. Money saved together, the first trip abroad together, that subject slowly raised in the kitchen, are still ahead of me. A shared past begins on the day you meet. It doesn't matter how old you are.

And that seaside town. Perhaps one day I really will go. There will be someone real beside me. Perhaps it will rain. The hotel will turn out bad. One evening we will fall out, then make up. Nothing will be as it was in my head.

But there will be one difference. It will really have been lived.

And a real memory is always warmer than a built one.

Until that day I will not let go of my dreams. But I will not let them ruin me either. Let them show me not what I have lost, but where I am going.
