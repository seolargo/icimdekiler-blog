---
baslik: Let a Door Open for Me, Just Once
---
Think of an evening. The city lights have just come on. The phone is on the table, its screen dark. You are looking at it, but you do not know that you are actually waiting for something. Not just a message. Not a name. What you are waiting for is for someone to have thought of you.

For years a person knocks on doors. Writes, asks, offers, waits. Sometimes the door opens, sometimes it does not. And when it opens, as you step inside the same question always catches in your mind: if I had not knocked, would this door have opened anyway?

To be desired is for this question, one day, to be answered quietly.

One morning your phone buzzes and you have written nothing. But they have. Nothing important, perhaps. A sentence as small as "you crossed my mind today." Or a photo of something they saw on the way, with "I thought of you when I saw this" written beneath it. No one asked them for it. It answers no question. It simply walked toward you, from inside them.

In that moment something in your chest loosens. It is as if, for a moment, you had set down a load you have carried for a long time. Because for the first time you feel you are not writing this story alone.

We do not talk about this much. Men especially never talk about it. We were always taught to walk forward. You approach, you ask, you don't insist but don't give up either. You bear rejection, get up, try again. But no one ever told us this: you too have the right to be waited for. You too have the right to be the one someone looks for with curiosity in their eyes.

To be desired is someone's eyes lingering a little longer when they look at you. It is them turning back a step as you part to ask, "shall we meet again?" It is them being sad when you cancel a meeting and suggesting another day right away. It is a knee touching yours under the table and them not pulling it away. It is a hug lasting a second longer than expected.

These are small things. But people live on these small things. Big words are forgotten. A hug that lasted a second longer is not.

After years of being the one who wants, a person begins at some point to doubt themselves. Maybe something is missing in me, they say. Maybe I am not someone to be wanted, they say. At night, lying in bed, they stare at the ceiling and tell this thought to no one. They do not tell because they are ashamed. Because they think it is shameful to want to be wanted.

It is not shameful. Wanting to be someone's eagerness is the oldest desire in the world. A child wants to see joy in their mother's face. A friend wants to be the one who is called. And a lover wants the one they love to come running to them. There is no weakness in this. There is only humanity.

But I know one thing too. Desire cannot be begged for. It is something that moves further away the harder you try to win it. However hard you try, if there is no spark for you inside someone, you cannot light that spark. And that is not your fault. No one burns for everyone. No one can be everyone's fire.

So perhaps the point is not to knock on every door. It is to knock on one door, then step back and wait. To listen for footsteps coming from behind it. If they do not come, not to wear yourself out in front of that door. To go on walking. Because somewhere, perhaps on a street you never expected, there may be someone waiting for your footsteps.

And when that day comes, when that door opens, when for the first time you step through a door you did not knock on, all those lonely evenings will have a meaning. You will understand that each of them was a step that brought you to that threshold.

Perhaps that day your phone will again be lying on the table. Its screen will again be dark. But you will no longer be waiting. Because you will know that somewhere, right now, someone is thinking of you.

This is what it is to be desired. To know that inside someone in this world there is a small place that is yours. And that it was not you who opened that place, but them.
