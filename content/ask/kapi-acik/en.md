---
baslik: The Door Is Open
---
I found you on a night when I thought I would never see a woman's youth. You, whom I don't yet know, whose name I don't know, whose face I have never seen. For hours I mourned your twenties. I thought that I could not be your first love, that your first holiday was spent with someone else, that when you first opened your whole heart to someone, it was not me standing before you. It hurt a great deal.

Then at some point I stopped. I looked again at the scene I had imagined.

There was a girl in that scene who loved very much. She had bought a gift with her first money and was holding out the package with trembling hands. At a bus station, at the crack of dawn, she was seeing off someone leaving for military service. Her eyes were full but she was smiling. She would wait for him to come back. She would love him, try to make it work with him, go on their first holidays with him. They would sit side by side on the sand, saying nothing.

And the boy being seen off in that scene was me.

Then I understood. It was not your youth I was mourning, but my own. Saying I had missed your past was easier than saying my past had gone by with no one in it. The first is a loss out there. The second is a room inside me. A room whose door I had not opened in a long time.

In that room are my twenties. Dances no one watched. Songs no one listened to. Streets I walked alone at midnight, joys I told no one about, tears no one wiped away. At one point I had decided to hide these from you. I wouldn't dance with you, I wouldn't play you my music. Because they were mine alone. When I said this I thought I was drawing a boundary. It turns out I was only naming my loneliness. Those years were mine alone because no one else was there.

Now I want to tell you this. While I wait for you.

Perhaps I won't see your twenties. I couldn't be beside you on the days you looked in the mirror and didn't like yourself. I couldn't stroke your hair at your first heartbreak. I couldn't hear you laughing on a balcony one summer evening without a thought in your head. This is a loss, I know. I won't pretend it isn't.

But you didn't see mine either. No one saw me off at a bus station, and you weren't there either. We will both come to each other with an incomplete story. In both our pockets there will be a few photographs never shown to anyone.

Maybe it will go like this. One evening you show me an old photo of yourself. You're twenty-one, your hair longer, your eyes shyer. I look at that girl and something in me aches. I wish I had been there, I say. You laugh. Good thing you weren't, you say, I was so silly back then. I love that silly girl anyway. Without ever having seen her.

Then I open the door of my own room to you. Slowly. I play you a song I wrote alone. I show you a dance step, in the kitchen, when no one is watching. Those years become mine again, but no longer alone. Because you listen to them. You didn't see my twenties, but you hear their echo.

Perhaps I can't be the first; you will be my first, but I won't be yours. I can't promise I won't think about this imbalance every night. Some nights it will come again. But now I know where it comes from. It doesn't come from you. It comes from my years that passed with no one.

And you don't have to pay the bill for those years. You only have to come.

My youth isn't over yet. I'm twenty-seven. If someone comes now, they will see my twenties. Perhaps you are that someone. Perhaps you'll be late and find me in my thirties. It doesn't matter. Whatever age you come at, I will give you the key to that room.

Because wanting to be loved is nothing to be ashamed of. Not having been loved like this when I was young does not mean I am not worth loving. It only means that love was still on its way.

And I am here. I am leaving the door open.
