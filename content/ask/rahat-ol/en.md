baslik: Relax
---
There are two words people hear most and understand least: relax. They say it before an exam, before a first date, at the door of a doctor's office. As if it were as easy as pressing a button. Yet relaxing is not a threshold you decide on in a moment and cross. It is something learned anew every day.

For a long time I could not relax. When someone smiled at me, the first thing I thought was why that smile had come to me. Why now, why me, was there no one else left? When a woman at the table looked at her phone and said "my ex texted," my face fell. The steam was still rising from the tea, but I had already sat down at another table, at her past. There were handsome men there, there was laughter, there were nights I knew nothing about. And at that table I could find no place for myself.

Then I realised that no one had invited me to that table. I had walked there on my own feet. The woman in front of me was still here, in Moda, in this small café where the wind was tossing her hair. She was talking to me. She was curious about my day. But I couldn't hear her, because the inside of my head was too loud.

Fear is a strange thing. It comes to protect you, but in the end it builds the very thing you feared with its own hands. You are afraid of being the backup, so you pull back. Someone who sees you pull back thinks you have gone cold, and they drift away too. Then you turn and say to yourself, "see?" "I was only ever the backup." But it was you who closed the door.

Relax, I tell myself now. But this does not mean feel nothing. There will still be a stirring inside you. You hear a name, a message arrives, your heart begins to stutter for a moment. Relaxing is not handing the whole evening over to that stutter. "All right," you say inwardly. "This feeling has come. Let it sit in a corner." Then you turn and tell the person across from you how boring today's meeting was. You laugh and say you lost your weekend. And as they laugh, you see that something has changed.

Because beside a relaxed person, everyone becomes a little more themselves. No one has to defend themselves. No one has to account for their past. The woman drops her phone into her bag and shows you a photo of a village breakfast. A garden with a sea view and wooden tables. "I'm stealing you away," she says. And for the first time you feel that you are not chasing someone, that someone is coming toward you. "You're so relaxed tonight," she says. "I like it." In that moment you understand that you found what you were looking for not by forcing, but by letting go.

It turns out you don't have to compete to be loved. I learned this late. I knocked on a hundred doors, most did not open. Of those that did, some later slowly closed. For a long time I drew this conclusion from it all: I am not someone to be wanted. But perhaps the right conclusion was something else. Perhaps I had knocked on the wrong doors, at the wrong hours, with tense hands. Perhaps for a door to open, I first had to relax.

Relaxing is not arrogance. It is not saying "I need no one." On the contrary, it is saying "I need you, but I won't hold you by force." It is seeing a person's past not as a threat but as the road they walked. It is noticing that at the end of that road, this evening, on this bench, they are sitting beside you. When they lean their head on your shoulder, not questioning who they are, just feeling that weight.

And sometimes the answer is "no." Someone tells you, "there's nothing between us." Relaxing is being able to hear this too. Without slamming the door, without making yourself small, simply being able to say "I understand." Their not wanting you does not mean you are not worth wanting. It only means they are not your road. And when you accept this, the world is still there, still wide.

One day someone will come. Perhaps their past will be crowded, perhaps quiet. Perhaps they won't notice you the moment they first see you, and you'll cross their mind one evening years later. None of this will matter. What will matter is what kind of person you are when they come. Someone tense, on guard, searching every sentence for a threat, or someone beside whom it is possible to breathe.

I am learning to relax. Slowly. My shoulders still sometimes tense on their own, out of old habit. My mind still sometimes goes and sits at that old table. But now I notice. And each time I notice, I tell myself the same two words. Relax. Even if no one comes. Even if everyone comes.

Perhaps what we call love is not catching someone, but being a place peaceful enough that they will want to stay.
