---
baslik: The Crystals Are Ours
---
Sometimes I think I love a woman. Then one night, staring at the ceiling in the dark, I understand. It was not her I loved, but her picture. A picture living in my head, drawn by me, coloured by me.

How beautiful that picture was. It never got tired. It never got angry. It never shut the door on me on a bad day. Whenever I wanted, it was there. I had tuned its voice, I had chosen its smile, I had written how it would look at me. I had taken only a few pieces from a real person. A walk, a glance, a sentence. I had filled in the rest. For years, quietly, without telling anyone.

Stendhal told of this long ago. In Austria there were salt mines. In winter they would throw a bare tree branch down into them. When they took it out a few months later, the branch was no longer a branch. It was covered from end to end in shining salt crystals, dazzling in the light. To Stendhal, falling in love was just this. The person we love is that bare branch. The more we lay our own dreams, our own longings, our own lacks upon it, the more it shines.

But the crystals are not theirs. The crystals are ours.

When you realise this, something breaks inside. As if for years I had been embracing emptiness. As if all those nights, all those thoughts, had been for nothing. But then I see something else. Everything I put into that picture was real. My wish to be loved was real. My wish to be chosen by someone, to be special in someone's life, to no longer be alone, was real. The picture may have been false, but the longing was not. Only its address was wrong.

Loving the image is easy. Because the image never surprises you. You wrote it. You know what it will say, you know what it will want. You never argue with it, you never make up, because you never fall out. Thinking of it is sweeter than talking to it. You are even a little afraid of meeting it as it really is. In case the picture breaks.

Loving a woman is something else. Before you stands a person independent of you. You did not write her. How she wakes in the morning, what quietly saddens her, which song brings tears to her eyes, you learn only by living. She surprises you. She says something you did not expect, wants something you did not think of. Sometimes she gets on your nerves. Sometimes you do not understand her. But you want her anyway. You wonder not what she will make you feel, but how she feels. You want her to be happy, even if nothing of it is left for you.

That is where the difference lies. Loving the image is dressing a woman's face in your own longing. Loving a woman is wanting the person behind that face as she is, even if she is different from what you imagined.

Perhaps everyone begins like this. Perhaps every love begins a little with a picture. In the first days you think of her constantly, you do not see her flaws, you load great meanings onto a small message. This is not a crime. This is how people love, how it begins. But then one day the picture begins to crack. The real person shows through the crystals. And in that moment one of two things happens. Either love ends, because what was loved was only the picture. Or love deepens, because the real person turns out more beautiful than the picture.

I learned one more thing. Seeing someone as a little more beautiful than they are is not a bad thing. People who stay happy for many years still look at their partners with a little admiration. But for them the picture did not take the place of the real. It was added on top of the real. First they came to know the person, then they lit their own lights for that person.

My picture never met the real. The crystals grew and grew, but the branch never came out of the mine. That is why it stayed so bright. That is why it lasted so long. That is why letting go is so hard.

Now I am trying to let it go. Not by breaking it. Not by belittling it. Only by putting it there, where it belongs. As a picture I once loved very much, one that did not leave me alone on very long nights.

And one day someone will appear before me. Without crystals, bare, as she is. She will surprise me. Perhaps at first I will not even recognise her. But this time I will not draw a picture. I will wait, I will listen, I will learn.

Because where the image ends, the person begins.
