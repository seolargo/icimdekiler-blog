---
baslik: The Old Rooms of the Heart
---
Everyone who comes to a lover brings an invisible suitcase. Inside are the sounds of their childhood. A mother's embrace that came too late, a father's slammed door, a night of crying that never ended. The lover does not see that suitcase. They see only a smiling face. But as love goes on, the suitcase's zip slowly opens.

They say that how a person will love is hidden in how they were loved when very small. If someone came when they cried, if they were held when someone came, that child learns to trust the world. Grown up, they reach for a lover's hand without fear. But if love sometimes came and sometimes did not, the heart grows up waiting. With every late message it thinks a door is closing. If love never came at all, the heart learns to be enough for itself. As closeness grows, it wants to open a window, to breathe.

The hardest is this: for some children, the arms they would run to for shelter are also the arms they fear. When you are afraid you want to run, but you cannot run to the fear itself. When that child grows up and falls in love, they want both to come closer to the one they love and to flee from them. One night they say "stay," the next morning "go." Even a calm evening feels to them like the silence before a storm. Because in their heart, love and danger are written on the two sides of the same letter.

Perhaps that is why some loves break at their most beautiful point. Someone blows out the candle while it is still burning. Not out of malice, but out of fear. They are so afraid of losing that they choose to bring the loss about with their own hand. Peace is foreign to them; the storm is familiar. A voice whispers in their ear: "You don't deserve this." And they say they do not want the flower held out to them. What they refuse is not really the flower. It is the closeness the flower carries. And sometimes a need they cannot speak hides inside a small quarrel. Instead of saying "see me," they get hurt, sulk, pull away.

And some loves are lived without ever beginning. A face loved from afar, a hand never touched, words that arrive at midnight. In such a love, what is loved is most often not a person but a dream. We fill every gap we do not know with our own longing. We give the loveliest voice to someone whose voice we have never heard. Reality never comes to spoil the picture. That is why such loves never grow old. They stay forever in the brightness of the first day. Like an unfinished song, one sings its ending over and over in one's head.

You want to forget. But the more you try to forget, the more you remember. Perhaps the point is not forgetting. Perhaps one needs to say a gentle goodbye to that dream. To the seaside road never walked, the table never shared, the "good morning" never said. Like writing a letter, without sending it.

Then loneliness comes. If someone came, really came, we think everything would be fine. To a degree that is true. A real warmth puts out even the brightest dream. But looking at love as a saviour places too heavy a load on the shoulders of the one to be loved. A lover is not a doctor. They cannot dress our wounds; they can only hold our hand while we dress them. Perhaps what we should look for is not someone who saves us, but someone who walks beside us.

Sometimes the heart always goes to the same door. The locked one, the uncertain one, the unreachable one. Because uncertainty feels like excitement, and distance like passion. A calm love, by comparison, seems dim. Yet love is not always a spark. Sometimes it is the quiet warmth of a stove burning on a winter evening. It does not show off, but it keeps you warm until morning.

People always ask: are we drawn to those who are like us, or to our opposites? The opposite enchants. It carries what you do not have, sings a song you do not know. But in time that song wears you out. What lasts is likeness at the root. Valuing the same things, looking at the same horizon, wanting to be soaked by the same rain. The branches can differ. One loves mornings, the other nights. One talks, the other listens. As long as the roots drink from the same soil.

Perhaps love is two people being able to open their suitcases side by side. Being able to say, "Look, these are my wounds." And the other being able to sit and look at them without running away. No one can heal anyone completely. But two wounded hearts, around the same table, under the same light, can remind each other of healing.

And one day, after a very long wait, someone knocks on your door. This time they do not stop at the threshold. They take off their shoes, come in, and without any hurry, light a lamp in the oldest rooms of your heart.
