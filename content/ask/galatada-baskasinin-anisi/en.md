---
baslik: Another Love at Galata
---
One evening I said to her, let's go up the Galata Tower.

I didn't really want the tower. Seeing Istanbul from above didn't matter to me either. I wanted to live something with her. To climb the narrow stairs together, to stand against the wind at the top, to look side by side as the city lights came on. Years later, every time I saw that tower, I wanted to remember her.

So I wasn't offering a tower. I was offering a memory.

She said, I've already been up with someone, what's the point.

A little later she added: I won't go up there, whatever happens. I have a memory there with someone.

In that moment I understood that some places come to belong to people. For her Galata was not a tower. It was someone else's face. Someone else's voice. The wind of another evening. And however much I knocked on that door, that room would not open to me. Because someone was still sitting inside it.

How strange. For years I had longed for memories I had never lived. She had locked away a memory she had lived, so no one could touch it. We were both looking at the past. I at a past that never happened, she at a past that was over. And between us stood a present that neither of us was looking at.

That evening she also called me abi, big brother. A small word. Perhaps out of habit. But some small words measure the distance between two people better than great walls.

On the way home I thought. What wears a person out the most?

Having no eagerness left at all? Waking one morning wanting nothing, waiting for no one, feeling excited for no one. This is perhaps the deepest. Because not even pain is left behind, only an emptiness.

Not feeling it while the other person is eager? Someone loves you, waits, tries, and you cannot find that stirring inside you. This is heavy too. Instead of pain you carry guilt.

Or them not feeling it while you are eager?

I think this hurts the most. Because you both want and feel unwanted. Every day you knock on a door and the door opens halfway. You write, they answer, but they never start. You suggest a place, they find a reason, but they never suggest another place. And after a while a person turns to themselves and asks: is something missing in me?

But then I thought this too. This pain shows that something in me is still alive. A heart that wants can break, but it is still alive. A heart that has stopped wanting does not break, but it does not live either. I still want to climb a tower. I still want to stand against the wind beside someone. My eagerness is intact.

I know now that a relationship is not built without effort. No one will come to my door one morning and take me into a ready-made story. I will write, I will offer, I will make room. I am not running from these.

But giving effort and giving effort alone are not the same thing.

In a good relationship both people give effort. One suggests a tower, the other says "let's go." Or says, "not there, but come, let's go somewhere else together, let it be ours." One is hurt, the other makes amends. Effort like this does not wear you out. Everything you give comes back to you from somewhere. It even brings joy.

One-sided effort is like carrying love to a wall. You carry and carry, and the wall stays where it is. You get tired, the wall does not.

That evening we did not go up Galata. Perhaps we never will. And perhaps that is a good thing. Because I do not want to be a guest inside someone else's memory. I want my own memory. Not a bench no one has ever sat on, or a street no one has ever walked, perhaps. But a place not filled with anyone's memory. A place where a woman will say, "I have a memory there with you."

One day someone will open such a door for me. When I offer, she will take a step too. When I suggest a place, she will say "let's go," her eyes smiling. And that day the effort I give will feel not like a burden, but like a gift.

Until that day I will give my share. I will offer, I will show my interest, I will knock on the door. Then I will step back and look. Is a hand reaching out from the other side?

If it does, I will take it.

If it doesn't, I will take my effort to another door. If Galata has a memory, this city has thousands more streets. And one of them, one day, will be ours.
