baslik: Four Seasons
---
Being drawn to someone lasts no longer than a breath. A glance, a smile, a sentence left half-finished. The heart quickens, the mind keeps circling that face, the nights grow long and sleep will not come. In those first days we do not really know the other person. Every place we do not know, we fill with imagination. Perhaps what we first love is not that person, but the picture they leave inside us.

Then attraction is returned and love begins. Two separate walks merge into one road, and "I" quietly becomes "we." Everything still shines, but now a real person begins to appear from behind that picture. And one day, inevitably, the dream and the real meet face to face.

On that day the flawless person in our head gives way to the real one. Flaws appear, differences sharpen, the first hurts are felt. The fire dies down a little. Most loves end right at this threshold, because people mistake this stillness for a farewell. Yet it is not a farewell but a turning of the season. Those who cross the threshold build another kind of bond. The racing of the heart gives way to something deeper: being able to say "I am at ease with you." Then comes commitment, and it is no longer a feeling but a decision. Two people choose each other with their eyes open. As the years pass, love comes to lean on friendship, on respect, on a past gathered together. It draws its strength not from the flame but from the embers.

Inside a love there are really three things: desire, closeness and the decision to stay. If there is only desire, it is a summer fire, quick to catch, quick to turn to ash. If desire has gone but closeness and faithfulness remain, it is a quiet friendship. When all three are there together, that is when love is whole. But these three do not stand on their own. They ask to be built again every day.

This journey takes not months but years. Nor are its stops divided by sharp lines. They blend into one another and sometimes turn back. In loves born of friendship, attraction and commitment come in through the same door. For lovers who are far apart the road grows longer, because getting to know someone in the middle of real life is delayed.

The old ones knew this. To know someone, they used to say, you must spend four seasons with them. In the first months everyone appears at their most beautiful. But it is hard to carry that for long, and within a year the mask slowly slips. At holiday tables, among their family, you see who they are. When life tightens around them you learn whether they stay calm or turn their anger on you. When you are ill you notice whether they are at your bedside or disappear. When plans fall apart on a journey, when they are worn out, when you spend long days alone together, you learn who they are. You see how they turn inward in winter, and the sun inside them in summer. And in the first great storm it becomes clear whether they can apologise, whether they can take the first step toward making peace. Four seasons is not a rule. But it reminds us that most of a lifetime is woven not of great moments but of ordinary and difficult days.

Of course, not everyone waits four seasons. Some marry after a short acquaintance. Some are introduced by a family, and because values and surroundings are known in advance, the road is kept short. Some feel they are ready now and do not want to wait. And some say, "I knew the moment I saw them." This road carries a risk, because a person does not yet fully know whom they have chosen. But homes built quickly that stay happy for a lifetime are not few. So speed alone does not write the fate of a love.

The real difference is in the order. Those who marry quickly pass through the same seasons. It is only that the meeting of dream and reality, the first hurts and the four seasons come not before the wedding but after it. The things that make those seasons lighter are clear: believing in the same things, not expecting perfection from each other, a family standing behind them, and seeing marriage not as a feeling but as labour. Those who pass through these seasons with patience put down roots over time. For those who cannot, the first winter can be very harsh.

In the end, whether the road is long or short, it arrives at the same question. Attraction opens the door, love walks in, commitment builds the house. But living in that house is possible only by staying on as the seasons turn. The real point is not to keep the excitement alive forever. It is being able to choose each other again, when the excitement has stilled, when winter is at the door.
