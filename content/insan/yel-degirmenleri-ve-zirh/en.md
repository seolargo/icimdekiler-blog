baslik: Windmills and Armour
---
There is an old man on the stage. He is wearing tin armour and holding a rusty lance. Everyone laughs at him. He takes the windmills before him for giants, an ordinary innkeeper's daughter for a princess. They call him mad. But he turns and looks at them all, as if to say: true madness is seeing the world only as it is and bowing to it.

I think about that tin armour. When Don Quixote put it on, he believed himself strong. But the armour did not protect him. He fell, he was wounded, he was mocked. Still he got up. What made him strong was not the armour. It was getting back on the horse every time he fell.

We men are given armour too, when we are small. Don't cry, stand straight, don't let it show. A real man doesn't break, they say. And we put that armour on. We don't take it off for years. One day we notice we cannot breathe inside it. No one can touch us, yes. But we cannot touch anyone either.

No one ever told us what being strong means. They only said: be strong.

I think a strong man is one who carries his own load on his own back. He gets tired, but he does not throw his load at anyone's feet. He gets sad, angry, afraid. He lives these things and does not hide them. But his feelings do not decide in his place. Perhaps he falls apart one night; in the morning he pulls himself together again. Though he is not sure, he chooses a road. If he is wrong, he says "my mistake." He does not shrink for saying it. On the contrary, that is the moment people begin to trust him.

A strong man can say "no." Without shouting, without slamming doors. He simply says "this isn't good for me" and gets up and leaves. He can be alone. Loneliness pains him, but it does not destroy him. He wants someone, misses them, loves them. But when that person leaves, his life does not collapse. He knows the fine line between wanting and needing.

And there is the other man. We know him too. Perhaps at times we have been that man. The man who always leans on someone to carry his life. Who breaks apart when difficulty comes and waits for someone else to gather up the pieces. Who cannot decide, and then blames others for everything that goes wrong. Who loses himself trying to please everyone. Who clings to the woman he loves, because he thinks that if he lets go he will fall. And the more he clings, the more he smothers. That man does not share his weakness. He loads it on. One day the woman he loves stops being his lover and becomes someone who looks after him. And she grows tired of looking after him.

But this is not what I really want to say.

What I really want to say is this: being strong is not never breaking. No such person exists. The man who says he has never cried, never been afraid, is only wearing thicker armour. No one knows what is inside it. Perhaps not even him.

There is a third man. I think he is the strongest.

He too stands on his own feet. But one day, beside a woman, he slowly takes off his armour. Not at once. Not from the first day. Piece by piece, as trust grows. When he is tired he says, "I'm so tired." When he is afraid he says, "I'm afraid." And then he still knocks on the door, still goes to work, still tries. He tells her his trouble but does not expect her to carry it. He only wants her beside him. And one day she takes off her own armour too. Because opening up is not something one person does alone. Two people are a little defenceless at the same time. Perhaps that is exactly what we call intimacy.

The weak man loads his burden onto the other. The strong man carries his burden himself. But the truly strong man learns to carry his burden beside someone.

I come back to Don Quixote. Everyone thought he was defeated. He swung his lance at the windmills and fell to the ground. But at the end of the story, what people remembered was not his falling but his rising, every time.

Perhaps we are like that too. Perhaps our strength lies not in never falling, but in being able to rise without hiding that we fell.

The man who can take off his armour is braver than the one who never does. Because he has chosen to show his wound and still remain standing.
